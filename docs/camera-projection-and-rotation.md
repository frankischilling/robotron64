# Projection, reflection highlights and Euler rotation

Six SDK procedures recover perspective projection, look-at/highlight
generation, Euler rotation and their fixed-matrix wrappers. A seventh game
procedure supplies the float-angle interface used by frame projection.
Together they account for 3,240 live code bytes, three complete `.rodata`
sections totaling 64 bytes, and four live bytes of private static storage.

## Projection and the game angle interface

`func_80060750` initializes a float matrix, converts the field of view to
radians, and calculates the half-angle cotangent. It writes the perspective
terms, scales all sixteen entries, and optionally stores the RSP perspective
normalization value. A near/far sum at or below two produces `0xFFFF`; the
other branch narrows the computed value to an unsigned short and raises a
zero result to one. `func_80060980` builds that float matrix on its stack and
uses the recovered fixed-point matrix conversion.

The game's `func_8004CE08` truncates its two float inputs to integers, calls
the existing scaled angle routine `func_8003CD4C`, and converts the result
back to float. Frame projection uses it to turn its height/distance input into
a field of view. Its complete 104-byte body reproduces the MIPS-I floating
control-register save, rounding-mode selection and restoration generated by
the original casts. Providing this source also supplies the previously missing
production link dependency of `frame_projection.c`.

## Camera and light records

The camera header reuses the existing 64-byte `SdkMatrix` type. It defines and
checks the SDK's 16-byte aligned light record, 32-byte look-at record and
16-byte highlight record. Direction components occupy offsets eight through
ten in each light; the surrounding color and duplicate-color fields retain
their byte widths.

The highlight routine normalizes the look, right and up vectors, normalizes
each of two light directions, and computes the halfway vectors used for the
highlight coordinates. Each halfway-vector length is compared with the
original double constant `0.1`. The fallback coordinates and the order of
float operations are preserved. It writes the reflection direction bytes
using the target's double-precision multiplication by 128 and upper clamp at
127, then writes both light colors and the camera matrix. The second procedure
converts the resulting float matrix through the same shared matrix converter.

## Euler angles and static storage

The rotation routine converts roll, pitch and yaw from degrees, obtains their
sines and cosines, initializes the matrix, and fills its rotation terms.
Its wrapper converts that matrix to the fixed representation.

The degrees-to-radians value retains its source-static initialized float
declaration. IDO 5.3 emits a constant load and a store to the corresponding
private BSS slot at `0x80194D10`, despite the mathematical expression being
constant. The emitted object's sixteen-byte BSS extent is checked, while
only the four-byte `degreesToRadians` symbol is counted as live storage. Its
private ECOFF name and offset are verified before the alignment tail is
trimmed for integration.

The complete generated constant sections are 16 bytes at `0x80095CF0`,
32 bytes at `0x80095D00`, and 16 bytes at `0x80095D20`. They include the
compiler's natural section alignment and are compared in full against ROM
offsets `0x968F0`, `0x96900`, and `0x96920`. No source padding declarations
are used. Natural trailing text alignment is excluded from the code counts.

## References and validation

Robotron's complete instructions establish the interfaces, field offsets,
arithmetic precision, branch conditions and procedure boundaries. The pinned
libreultra `perspective.c`, `lookathil.c` and `rotateRPY.c`, together with the
007 GU header variant, corroborate the SDK algorithms and declarations.
The online `rotateRPY.c` confirms the source-static initialized degree
constant; `lookathil.c` confirms the highlight threshold and fallback formulas.
Their use and revisions are recorded in [CREDITS.md](../CREDITS.md).

The three SDK units match IDO 5.3 with `sdk-o3-mips2-r4300-mul`; the game
angle wrapper matches the `game` profile. Complete `camera-target-v1` and
`runtime-angle-target-v1` comparisons verify every procedure and allocated
section. The production comparisons also include the frame-projection caller
after replacing its local prototypes with the recovered shared interfaces.
The [provenance record](camera-projection-and-rotation-provenance.json) retains
the accepted source and header hashes, compiler identity, complete procedure
extents, allocated section placements and candidate and canonical report
identities. All five source units, including the existing frame caller,
passed the registered complete comparison.
